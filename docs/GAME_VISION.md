# Game Vision

## Working title

**Merge Discovery**

The title is provisional, but accurately expresses the current interaction: combine known things to discover new things.

## High concept

A cozy discovery game where the player begins with the most primitive concepts of existence and gradually reconstructs an increasingly rich universe through experimentation.

The game initially feels logical and almost scientific: void, energy, matter, stars, planets, geology, water, life, plants and animals.

As the player's world becomes richer, previously impossible combinations begin to make sense and hidden domains emerge: folklore, magic, dreams, spirits, paradoxes, impossible places and stranger concepts.

## Player promise

> “I wonder what happens if I combine these two.”

Every major system should reinforce that impulse.

## What the game is

- A combination/discovery game.
- A collection game.
- A progression game.
- A mystery game.
- A curated universe of relationships between concepts.
- Web-first, responsive and designed from the beginning for touch interfaces.

## What the game is not

- A classic merge ladder where two identical objects become a higher-level version.
- A clicker.
- An idle economy game.
- A game built around stamina or waiting timers.
- A procedural text generator where arbitrary pairs can produce arbitrary results.
- A brute-force matrix where the player is expected to test every possible pair.

## Core emotional rhythm

1. Curiosity.
2. Experiment.
3. Reaction.
4. Reveal.
5. Recognition or surprise.
6. Collection progress.
7. New possibility.
8. “One more combination.”

## Content philosophy

Canonical discoveries are authored and curated.

Procedural or generative tools may assist design, auditing and expansion, but they should not decide canonical recipes during runtime.

The universe should feel as though it has an internal logic even when it becomes fantastical.

## Tone

Cozy, curious, intelligent, lightly magical.

The game should gradually move from comprehensible natural systems into wonder and impossibility without feeling like two unrelated games stitched together.

## Long-term potential

The architecture should support:

- hundreds or thousands of elements;
- new sets/domains without engine changes;
- secret content;
- multiple valid recipes for one discovery;
- later expansion of experiment types;
- seasonal or thematic content if desired;
- web/PWA distribution and later Android packaging.
